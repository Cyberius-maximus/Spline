One call each morning. With its reasons attached.

Spline compares last night with your own normal range, not a population average, and turns it into a decision you can act on before breakfast.

Green light, ease off, swap to easy or rest, with the measurement that decided it right underneath. On a morning without enough data, it makes no call at all.

Features

Everything your data can tell you. Nothing it can’t.

Sleep, recovery, training, food, your gut and the long game. Each one built on a named method you can look up.

Sleep, and a plan for tonight

A sleep score built on regularity, duration and efficiency, the parts the research supports. Then tonight’s plan, worked back from your own nights: wind-down, last coffee, lights out.

Your normal, not the population’s

HRV and resting heart rate measured against a 60-day range that’s yours alone. One low morning is a signal. A run of them is a pattern.

Every workout, analysed

Heart-rate zones, strain and recovery for each recorded session, with a share card that shows only what you choose.

Fitness, fatigue and form

A named training-load model, plus an HRV gate that can veto a hard day when your body disagrees with the plan.

Describe a meal. Check the numbers.

Type what you ate, scan a barcode or a label. Calories come from food databases or the label itself, never invented by the AI, and Spline learns from your corrections.

Gut health, counted, not guessed

Fiber against 30 g a day, 30 different plants a week and fermented foods, all counted from what you already log, plus an optional daily check-in. Each lever is graded by its evidence. There’s no microbiome score, because no validated one exists.

An early warning, honestly framed

Five overnight vitals, each against your own range. One odd night never raises it. It takes two vitals out together, or one staying out night after night. A hard session, last night’s drinks or your cycle phase are named and discounted.

  1. Early signs of strainTwo or more vitals outside your range on one night, the same one outside two nights running, or wrist temperature strongly raised.
  2. Your body may be fighting somethingThree or more outside on one night, two or more on two nights running, or early signs three nights running.

An early warning, never a diagnosis. Its baseline leaves out the two most recent nights, so a cold that’s already building can’t become its own normal.

Biological age, taken apart Pro

The age your vitals read as, with the years each factor adds or subtracts. Labelled as an estimate, because it is one.

A monthly review from your own data Pro

A scorecard against the month before, what moved most, and a chart for every section, ranked by how far each one moved. Then play the month back as story slides.

Vitals, each against its normal

Breathwork, eyes shut

Ask Spline

Ask why. Get your numbers, with their sources.

Ask about your readiness, sleep or training in plain words. Spline answers from your own data and the research behind each metric, on your iPhone. If it hasn’t measured something, it says so.

Meet Splino, the little spline that answers. Modelled and animated in Blender, it looks down while you type, looks up while it reads your numbers and talks while the answer appears. It’s there for company only: it never changes what an answer says.

  • The model picks the topic. It never writes the numbers. The on-device model only works out what you’re asking. The answer is built from the same values your cards show, so the two can’t disagree.
  • Every suggestion names its study. “Breathe slowly, daily” arrives with Laborde 2022, PMID 35623448. Change one thing at a time and let your own numbers judge it.
  • Works without the cloud. No chatbot server, no account. Without Apple Intelligence it still works, with a built-in keyword router.

Built on rules

Six rules Spline won’t break.

Trust lives in the details. These aren’t slogans; they decide what the app is allowed to show you.

  1. No alert without its reason.

    Every warning carries the measurement that triggered it. The app has no way to raise one without it.

  2. Silence beats a false all-clear.

    Missing data is named, never filled in. With no HRV this morning, Spline makes no call rather than guessing one.

  3. Your normal, not an average.

    Baselines are your own 60-day ranges, so a “good” number means good for you, not for a stranger.

  4. “Associated”, never “caused”.

    Habit patterns are tested against the next morning, corrected for multiple comparisons, and reported as associations.

  5. The AI never owns a number.

    On-device models read your words and pick the topic. Every value comes from a measurement, a food database or the label you scanned.

  6. Your data is never paywalled.

    Export everything as JSON or CSV, free, forever. Pro pays for extra analysis, never for access to what you recorded.

Questions

Fair questions. Straight answers.

What is Spline?

Spline is a health and training app for iPhone, iPad and Apple Watch; on the App Store it’s called Spline Health. It reads what your devices already record in Apple Health, such as HRV, resting heart rate, sleep and workouts, and turns it into one clear call each morning: push, ease off or rest. Every score shows what moved it and cites the study behind it by PubMed ID.

Do I need to buy a band or ring?

No. Spline has no hardware of its own. It reads Apple Health, so it works with an Apple Watch, Oura, Garmin, Withings, Amazfit or Polar device. The morning call is built on overnight HRV, so a device that records HRV gives you the most. An Apple Watch covers HRV, sleep and workouts best; Garmin doesn’t share HRV with Apple Health.

Is Spline free?

Yes. Today’s call, sleep, recovery, training, the food log, gut health, the early illness warning, your year in review and full JSON and CSV export are free, with no account and no trial timer. Spline Pro costs €3.99 a month or €34.99 a year, with a first month at €0.99 on the monthly plan, and adds biological age, bloodwork import, experiments, adaptive planning, the monthly review and Ask Spline.

Where is my health data stored?

On your iPhone. Spline reads Apple Health and does its analysis on the device. There’s no account and no Spline server, so there’s nowhere else for your data to go, and the app has no analytics or advertising SDK. An optional Face ID or passcode lock keeps it to you.

How is the readiness score calculated?

Readiness starts at 100. HRV against your own baseline adds or subtracts the most; a resting heart rate above baseline, sleep under about 7 hours or over about 8.5, and a raised breathing rate or wrist temperature subtract. 80 and above reads Prime, 60–79 Good, 40–59 Moderate and below 40 Low. Every formula is on the Methods page.

Can Spline warn me before I get ill?

It can flag early signs. Spline watches five overnight vitals, resting heart rate, HRV, respiratory rate, wrist temperature and blood oxygen, each against your own range. One odd night never raises it: it takes two vitals out together, or one staying out night after night. A hard session, last night’s drinks or your cycle phase are named and discounted. It’s an early warning, never a diagnosis.

How is Spline different from other wearable apps?

Most wearable apps ship proprietary scores, keep your data on their servers and need their own band or ring. Spline names the formula and the PubMed study behind every score, says so on the metric when the evidence is weak, keeps your data on your iPhone, works with the devices you already own, and lets you export everything as JSON or CSV for free.

Does Spline use AI?

Only to understand your words. Apple’s on-device model reads what you type, such as a meal or a question, and picks the topic; it never writes a number. Values come from your measurements, food databases such as USDA and Open Food Facts, or the label you scanned. Without Apple Intelligence, built-in rules do the same job.

Is Spline a medical device?

No. Spline is an informational fitness and wellbeing tool. It isn’t a medical device under Regulation (EU) 2017/745 and doesn’t diagnose, treat or prevent any disease. Anything the app grades Clinical is a conversation for your doctor.

When can I get Spline?

Spline is launching soon on the App Store as Spline Health, for iPhone and iPad on iOS 17 or later, with an Apple Watch app. It’s fully translated into English and German.
